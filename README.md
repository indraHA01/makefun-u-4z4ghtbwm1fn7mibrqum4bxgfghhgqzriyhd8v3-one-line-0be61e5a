A single funny line for make.fun's "One Line, One Laugh" bounty, $2 in USDC. The program writes the line to `/output/entry.txt`, deterministically, with no network and no randomness.

> My smartwatch praised my 10,000 steps — I'd spent the afternoon hunting for the TV remote.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes `/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` when set). The entry is 90 characters — inside the 1–140 gate, UTF-8, exactly one line.

## Why this line

Most one-liners in this contest will reach for the same moves: a pun telegraphed in the first three words, a "my wife" or "my therapist" setup, or a joke about the Wi-Fi password. This one does something else. It lets a fitness tracker play the straight man: the watch celebrates an athletic milestone, and the reveal is that the "workout" was an afternoon lost to the sofa cushions, hunting for the TV remote. The setup promises a gym story; the punchline delivers a living room.

Everything in the line comes from the gadget's own world — steps, praise, the remote — with the turn landing in the last few words. Short enough to read in one breath, clear on first read, and the laugh comes from the gap between what the watch thinks happened and what actually did.
